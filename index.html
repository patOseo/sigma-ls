Definition

What a leadership assessment is, and what separates a valid one from a questionnaire.

The short answer

A leadership assessment provides a standardized way to understand the traits and tendencies that influence how someone approaches leadership. It is different from a performance review. Performance reviews look at how someone is doing in their current job. The LSP-R helps identify strengths and development priorities across a consistent set of 50 leadership competencies. For the individual, that creates a clearer picture of where to focus development. For the organization, it provides a common framework for comparing results against the requirements for different leadership roles.

01

It measures tendencies, not ability

The LSP-R does not measure how well someone is performing in their current role or directly test their ability in a particular skill. It measures the natural tendencies that influence how they approach 50 specific leadership competencies.

02

It reports competencies, not types

Instruments that sort people into categories are easy to read but difficult to act on. Being assigned a type does not tell someone what to develop or give them a clear way to track progress. The LSP-R reports results across 50 individual leadership competencies. Those same competencies can also be measured using 360-degree feedback, providing a consistent framework for development and measuring progress over time.

03

It supports development, not just self-awareness

Every competency measured by the LSP-R can be developed. The results give each participant a clear starting point by identifying their strengths and development priorities. Norm base and validity

A percentile is a claim. It has to be a claim about the right people.

The short answer

A norm group is the population a score is compared against, and it decides what a percentile actually means. The LSP-R compares each leader to senior managers and executives across industries, so the 50th percentile is the middle of a group already doing leadership work, not the middle of the general public.

The comparison group

Senior managers and executives

Scores are placed against people already doing leadership work, across industries. The 50th percentile is a real line: it means the middle of that population, not the middle of the general public.

Why it is a high bar

Scoring 40th is not failing

It means below the midpoint of an already selective group. This is why a result is a statistical statement rather than an impression, and why it holds up when somebody asks how you arrived at it.

The comparison group is deliberately senior. Comparing leaders with other people already doing leadership work provides a more relevant standard than comparing them with the general population. Sample composition, reliability, and validity evidence are published in the technical manual.

Setting the standard

Not every leadership role requires the same things.

Understanding competencies helps to know where to focus development. Understanding what a role requires gives the organization a standard to measure against.

The short answer

The LSP-R measures the same 50 leadership competencies for every participant, but not every competency is equally important for every role. Some competencies deserve more focus depending on the role, the organization, and what the person in that role needs to accomplish.

Competency benchmarks identify which competencies matter most and establish a standard for interpreting the results. For individuals, that means knowing where to prioritize development. For organizations, it means understanding how assessment results compare with what a particular role requires.

What it is

Competency benchmark

Competency benchmarks identify the leadership competencies that matter most for a specific organization, department, or role. By understanding the competencies critical for success, organizations can identify what makes a role unique and which competencies a successful incumbent in the role requires.

How it is built

Creating a benchmark

A role benchmark addresses two levels: what is required for effective leadership at an organization, and what is required for success in a specific position. A single worksheet brings those requirements together and provides a standard that can guide development for people preparing for the role.

If your organization already uses a competency model, the benchmark can incorporate your existing language and requirements. If not, SIGMA can help you build it. Once established, the benchmark can support decisions about succession planning, leadership development, promotion readiness, and development gaps.

Common questions about leadership assessment.

What is a leadership assessment?

A leadership assessment provides a standardized way to understand the traits and tendencies that influence how someone approaches leadership. Unlike a performance review, which looks at how someone is doing in their current job, a leadership assessment can help identify strengths and development priorities across a consistent set of leadership competencies.

The LSP-R measures tendencies across 50 leadership competencies. Those results can be used to guide individual development and, when compared with what a particular role requires, support decisions about readiness for that role.

What does the LSP-R measure?

The LSP-R measures leadership tendencies across 50 competencies in four areas: cognitive skills, interpersonal skills, personal qualities, and senior leadership skills.

Each competency is reported individually rather than combining the results into a type or single overall score. This gives leaders a more specific picture of their strengths and development priorities and helps them identify where to focus their development.

How is the LSP-R different from other personality assessments?

The LSP-R is a personality-based leadership assessment. Rather than reporting only broad personality traits or assigning someone a type, it measures tendencies across 50 specific leadership competencies.

That makes the results more directly useful for development. Leaders can see their strengths and development priorities at the competency level and use the same competency framework to guide development and measure progress over time.

How long does the LSP-R take?

The LSP-R takes about 25 minutes and is completed online, with no proctor or scheduling required. Groups are usually given a two-week window so it fits around people's calendars, and most participants finish within a few days.

Each participant takes the assessment once. Their results can then be used for development and compared with different competency benchmarks without requiring them to take the assessment again.

What is a norm group and why does it matter in leadership assessment?

A norm group is the population an assessment score is compared with. It provides context for understanding what a score means relative to other people in that group.

The LSP-R uses a norm group of senior managers and executives across industries. That means a participant's results are compared with people already doing leadership work rather than with the general population.

What is a success profile?

A SIGMA Success Profile™ defines the competencies that are important for success in a specific role. It creates a clearer picture of what the role requires and gives the organization a consistent standard for evaluating and developing people for that role.

Assessment results can then be considered alongside the Success Profile to identify where an individual's strengths and development priorities align with what the role requires.

Do you need a competency framework before running a leadership assessment?

No. The LSP-R measures the same 50 leadership competencies for every participant, so you do not need to create your own competency framework before using the assessment.

If your organization already uses a competency model, it can be incorporated into the benchmarking process. If not, SIGMA can help you establish a competency benchmark based on what leadership requires in your organization and the roles you are assessing.

Can a leadership assessment predict leadership potential?

A leadership assessment can provide useful evidence when evaluating leadership potential, but it should not be treated as a prediction of future performance on its own.

The LSP-R measures tendencies across 50 leadership competencies. Those results can be considered alongside role requirements, performance history, experience, and other relevant information when evaluating someone for future leadership opportunities.

Should we use the LSP-R or 360-degree feedback?

They provide different kinds of information and can be useful together. The LSP-R uses self-report data to measure a leader's tendencies across 50 leadership competencies. 360-degree feedback measures how the leader's behavior is experienced by people who work with them.

Because both can measure the same 50 competencies, organizations can use them within a consistent framework for development and measuring progress over time.

Who should take a leadership assessment?

Can leadership assessment results be used for selection and promotion decisions?

Yes. Leadership assessment results can provide useful evidence for selection and promotion decisions, alongside other relevant information about the candidate and the requirements of the role.

Because these decisions affect employment, the assessment process should be appropriate for the intended use and applied consistently to candidates being considered for the same role. The order matters: define what the role requires, then measure, then decide.

Common questions about starting a leadership development program.

How do you start a leadership development program?

Start by understanding where development is most needed rather than choosing development content in advance. Measuring a group of leaders across a broad set of competencies gives you evidence about their strengths and development priorities before you decide what to invest in.

It also establishes a starting point. After a period of development, you can measure again to see where results have changed over time.

Where should you begin with leadership development if you do not know what the problem is?

Begin with measurement rather than trying to decide in advance what development people need. A broad leadership assessment can give you evidence about strengths and development priorities across a group without requiring you to choose specific development topics first.

The results will not explain every leadership challenge or tell you what a particular role requires. They give you a clearer starting point for deciding where to look more closely and what to do next.

What should you measure first in leadership development?

If you do not yet know where development is needed, start with a broad measure of leadership competencies rather than focusing on one area in advance. The LSP-R measures tendencies across 50 competencies, giving you a wider view of strengths and development priorities across the group.

That broader view can reveal patterns you were not specifically looking for and help you decide where a closer look may be needed.

How do you know whether your managers need development?

Look for evidence rather than relying on a single signal. Performance challenges, recurring issues across teams, feedback from others, and changes in what leadership roles require can all point to areas worth investigating.

A leadership assessment adds another source of evidence. The LSP-R measures each leader's tendencies across 50 competencies, helping you identify strengths and development priorities at the individual level and patterns across a group. Those results can then be considered alongside what you already know about the organization and its leaders.

How many people do you need to measure for this to be useful?

Individual results can be useful even when only one person is assessed. Each participant receives a personalized Focus Report and 12 months of Clarience based on their own assessment results and development priorities.

Including more leaders can give you a broader picture of patterns across the organization. Where results are broken down by level, function, or another group, SIGMA reports those patterns only when there are enough participants in the group to support the comparison.

How long does it take to get results?

For this starting-point engagement, the process takes about four weeks from choosing the group to reviewing the findings with SIGMA. Participants are given a two-week window to complete the LSP-R, which takes about 25 minutes.

SIGMA then analyzes the group results, with a 60-minute session to review the findings, put them in context, and discuss what to focus on next.

What if the results do not show clear development priorities?

That is still a useful result. Measurement does not have to uncover a significant development need to provide value. If the group results do not show clear patterns that warrant attention, you have evidence that can help you decide whether leadership development should be the priority or whether you need to look elsewhere.

Do you need a competency framework before starting leadership development?

No. The LSP-R measures the same 50 leadership competencies for every participant, so you can begin measuring without first creating your own competency framework.

Is leadership development worth the investment?

It depends on what you are trying to improve and how you will know whether anything changed. Measuring before development gives you a baseline for understanding strengths and development priorities before you decide where to invest.

After a period of development, you can measure again to see where results have changed over time. That gives you evidence of development progress, although changes should not automatically be attributed to a single program or development activity.

Two questions, two plans. Emergency cover, and readiness over three years.

Prepared for

01  ·  The two questions

A board asking about succession is asking two things

The first is emergency cover. If a senior leader left tomorrow, who holds the seat, who decides, and what is said to staff, funders and regulators. This is a continuity question and it has a short horizon.

The second is readiness. Over three years, is the organization developing people capable of holding these seats permanently. This is a capability question and it has a different answer.

Most succession plans answer the first question and are presented as though they answer the second. Separating them was the first step in this work and it changed what was measured.

What this document does. It reports coverage against both questions, seat by seat, with the measured evidence behind each judgment. It does not tell the board what to fund or which risks to accept.

Three findings

Immediate

Chief Executive succession rests on one person

Concentration

Three successors are named. One measures at the 71st percentile against a 75th requirement and is a credible three-year candidate. The other two measure at the 52nd and 49th, with shortfalls across Strategic Planning, Vision and Organizational Spokesperson. The plan reads as three deep and measures as one.

Chief Operating Officer is the strongest position

Depth

Two named successors both exceed requirement, at the 82nd and 74th percentiles. This is the only seat with genuine depth, and one of the two is a plausible medium-term Chief Executive candidate on the measured evidence, which is not currently reflected in the plan.

04  ·  What the evidence supports

Where the measurement points, and what it cannot see

The board can say with evidence that continuity is covered for four of five seats. Three-year readiness is a narrower picture and one seat needs attention now.

What the measurement establishes

Emergency cover exists for four seats and does not exist for one. That is a factual gap and it does not require judgment to interpret. Whether it is urgent depends on the current incumbent's circumstances, which are not in the data.

Chief Executive succession is measurably thinner than the plan describes. Whether one credible three-year candidate is acceptable is a matter for the board's risk appetite rather than for this document.

The number of names in a plan and the depth behind them are different quantities. This review can report the second; it is the first that has been reported to the board previously.

What this assessment cannot see. It has no view of incumbents' intentions or health, of individual successors' willingness to take a seat, of relationships with funders and regulators, or of the sector conditions the organization will face. It cannot tell the board how likely any departure is, which is the variable that determines how much of this matters.

05  ·  Method, and what this does not tell you

How to read this document

What was done

Requirements were recorded per seat before assessment. All eleven named successors completed the LSP-R within a two-week window and were scored against the requirements for the seat they are named for. Every participant received their own report and twelve months of coaching access.

What a percentile means here

Scores are positions against SIGMA's norm group of senior managers and executives across industries. The comparison group is people already doing leadership work, so the 50th percentile is the midpoint of an already selective population.

What this does not tell you

This is one input into the board's view of succession risk, alongside incumbent tenure and intentions, sector conditions, and what the executive team knows about the individuals. A score is a measured position at a point in time, not a ceiling.

Limits worth stating

The instrument measures capability against role requirements. It does not measure current performance, and it does not observe behavior as colleagues experience it. Where that view is wanted, a 360 against the same competency framework at twelve months provides it, and produces a movement reading against this baseline.

01  ·  The question

Two situations that look identical from the outside

A senior consultant was promoted to team lead fourteen months ago after six years of exceptional individual delivery. Since the promotion, two team members have left, project handovers have been inconsistent, and the manager is working longer hours than before.

There are two versions of this and they present the same way. In the first, the person has what the role needs and has never been taught the parts that are new to it. In the second, what the role requires runs against the profile the person actually has. The first closes with development. The second does not, and a year spent assuming otherwise costs the person as much as it costs the firm.

What this document does. It establishes which of the two situations is present, using measurement rather than another two quarters of observation. It does not decide what happens to this manager.

03  ·  What the evidence shows

Three gaps that move, one that may not

Delegation and developing others are teachable gaps

Developable

Seventeen and fourteen points below requirement respectively, on competencies that respond well to structured development where the underlying disposition supports it. Nothing in this profile works against either. These are the two most common gaps in newly promoted managers and the two most reliably closed.

Conflict Management is a different order of gap

Substantial

Twenty-nine points below requirement, at the 31st percentile, and sitting alongside Emotional Control at the 44th. The two together describe someone who finds disagreement genuinely costly. That combination is not a training gap in the ordinary sense, and it is the most likely explanation for inconsistent handovers and the two departures.

The strengths are real and are being under-used

Opportunity

Analytical Orientation at the 91st percentile and Thoroughness at the 88th are exceptional and well above peer level. They are the competencies the previous role rewarded, and the current role gives them less room. That is a role design observation as much as a development one.

04  ·  What the evidence supports

Where the measurement points, and what it cannot see

This is the second situation more than the first, but not entirely. Two of the four gaps close with development. The third is the one that decides this.

What the measurement establishes

Three of seven requirements are met, and the four that are not divide unevenly. Delegation and Developing / Coaching Others are ordinary new-manager gaps with a normal development path. Conflict Management at the 31st percentile is not, and it is central to a role that exists partly to absorb friction between clients and delivery teams.

The evidence supports treating this as a role fit question rather than a training question. It does not support a conclusion about this person's capability generally, which is high, and it says nothing about their performance in the previous role, which was exceptional.

What this assessment cannot see. It has no view of how much conflict this particular team actually generates, whether the two departures were related to management at all, what support this manager was given on promotion, or whether they want the role. It cannot tell you how a reshaped role would perform in practice. Any of those could change the conclusion.

01  ·  The question

Two kinds of error, and only one of them is visible

The high potential list is built from manager nominations at the annual talent review. Nineteen names, refreshed each year, and the development budget follows it.

Nomination can be wrong in two directions. Someone can be on the list who should not be, which is expensive and eventually becomes visible. Someone can be missing who should be there, which is more expensive and never becomes visible at all, because nothing about the process would ever surface them.

Why the population matters. Assessing only the list can confirm or challenge names already held. It cannot find the second error. This review assessed the list and a wider eligible population against the same standard, which is the only design that can.

The standard applied

Nine competencies recorded by the executive team as indicating readiness for a role one to two levels above current position, at the 65th percentile.

03  ·  What the evidence shows

Three findings

Nine people meet the standard and were never nominated

Missed

Nine of the thirty-three assessed from the wider population meet the standard, several of them comfortably. Two exceed the median of the current list. On the evidence available before this review, none of them would have received development investment in the next cycle.

Nomination tracks visibility more than capability

Process

Operations and shared services holds two list places out of nineteen and four of the nine missed. Commercial lines holds eleven places and two of the nine. The pattern is consistent with nomination requiring somebody senior to have had reason to notice, which favours customer-facing and revenue-attached roles. It is not evidence of deliberate bias and it is a structural feature worth correcting.

The six on the list who do not meet the standard are not a uniform group

Mixed

Four fall short on two or three competencies and are within reach with development. Two fall short on six or more. Treating all six the same way, in either direction, would be a mistake, and the conversations required are quite different.

04  ·  What the evidence supports

Where the measurement points, and what it cannot see

The list is largely right and materially incomplete. The second of those is the finding worth acting on.

What the measurement establishes

Thirteen of nineteen nominations are corroborated. Nine people who meet the same standard were not nominated, and their distribution across business units follows visibility rather than capability. Both are measured facts.

What the evidence does not establish is that any of the nine should be added to the list. Measured capability is one component of potential. Experience, judgment under pressure, mobility and appetite are others, and none of them appear here.

What this assessment cannot see. It has no view of performance, of whether any individual wants a larger role, of personal circumstances affecting mobility, or of how each person is regarded by those who work with them. It cannot tell you whether the six who fall short were nominated for good reasons this review has no sight of.

01  ·  The question

You cannot have a gap without a standard

The organization is midway through a shift toward automated fulfilment and a smaller, higher-skilled site workforce. The executive team is confident this changes what leaders need to do, and had not written down what it changes them into.

Without that, development becomes a purchase made against a general impression, and nobody can say afterwards whether it worked. The first half of this engagement was therefore not measurement at all. It was recording what the roles will require.

05  ·  Method, and what this does not tell you

How to read this document

What was done

Two requirement sets, current and three-year, were recorded per role tier by the executive team before assessment. Thirty-seven leaders completed the LSP-R within a two-week window and were scored against both. Every participant received their own report and twelve months of coaching access.

What a percentile means here

Scores are positions against SIGMA's norm group of senior managers and executives across industries. The comparison group is people already doing leadership work, so the 50th percentile is the midpoint of an already selective population.

What this does not tell you

This is one input into decisions about development investment, alongside strategy confidence, timing, financial constraints and operational context. The three-year requirement is this team's judgment, not a measured fact. A score is a measured position at a point in time, not a ceiling.

Limits worth stating

The instrument measures capability against role requirements. It does not measure current performance, and it does not observe behavior as colleagues experience it. Where that view is wanted, a 360 against the same competency framework at twelve months provides it, and produces a movement reading against this baseline.

01  ·  The question

Two people per seat, known very unevenly

The combined organization has one seat for every function and two credible people for most of them. The acquiring team has worked with its own leaders for years. It has known the acquired leaders for eleven weeks.

Familiarity is not a fair basis for choosing, and everybody involved knows it. It is also extremely difficult to set aside without something to replace it, which is what this review provides.
